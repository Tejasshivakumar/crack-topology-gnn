  C1. Frontier results are on 50-epoch checkpoints, main results are 200-epoch

  C2. No comparison against prior published work

  You compare your models against each other and against heuristics. There is zero comparison against any published crack detection or structural graph analysis method. IEEE
  reviewers always ask: "How does your method compare to [specific published paper]?" Without this, the paper has no external benchmark context.

  Fix: Find 2–3 published papers on crack graph analysis or structural health monitoring with GNNs and compare your pipeline's metrics against their reported numbers on a shared
  dataset.

  ---
  C3. End-to-end pipeline was never tested

  Your paper claims a complete 3-stage pipeline (Segmentation → Graph → GNN). But Stage 3 was trained and evaluated on graphs built from ground truth masks, not from Stage 1's
  output. If your segmentation model misses crack pixels, those errors propagate into the graph and the GNN results will degrade. You have never measured how much.

  Fix: Run 10–20 images through all 3 stages consecutively. Report the end-to-end node AP versus the oracle (ground truth mask) node AP. The gap is your pipeline error budget.

  ---
  C4. No qualitative figures

  Every IEEE paper on visual/structural analysis requires qualitative results — actual images showing your model working. You have training curves and numbers but zero figures
  showing:
  - A crack image with its skeleton and graph overlaid
  - A frontier prediction: the isolated tip node and the predicted reconnection
  - A failure case where the model gets it wrong

  Fix: Run link_prediction/visualize.py on a few test graphs and generate these figures.

  M1. GAT and GCN excluded from multi-seed runs without justification

  M2. Masking fraction never ablated for the main results

  You use 20% masking for the node task. The ablation results that exist (sage/ablation_results.json) show performance across fractions 10%–50% — but only for SAGE, only from
  50-epoch checkpoints, and it was never reported anywhere. Why 20%? A reviewer will ask.

  Fix: The ablation data already exists. Write it up. Show that 20% is a reasonable middle-ground choice backed by your own sweep.

  ---
  M3. Segmentation comparison ran to only 50 epochs — may not be converged

  Your segmentation finding (plain ResNet34d beats Hybrid) is based on 50 epochs with early stopping patience=25. The plain model might simply have converged faster, while the
  hybrid model needed more epochs. You cannot claim "GNN bottleneck doesn't help" if the hybrid model was still training when you stopped.

  Fix: Check the early stopping logs. If the hybrid model stopped significantly before patience was exhausted, it converged. If it was still improving at epoch 50, you need more
  epochs for a fair comparison.

  M4. No segmentation comparison to published SOTA

  You compare 3 variants of your own architecture. There is no comparison to DeepCrack (the benchmark's own model), DeepLabV3+, or any other published crack segmentation method. On
  the DeepCrack dataset specifically, published results exist and your 0.722 IoU needs to be placed in context.

  Fix: Add at least one published baseline — DeepCrack's own reported metrics on the DeepCrack test set, compared against your 0.722. Even a table footnote citing the published
  number counts.

  ---
  M5. No edge feature importance ablation

  You claim edge features are responsible for GINE's +0.039 gain over SAGE. But you have 7 edge features (path length, euclidean distance, tortuosity, angle, avg/min/max thickness).
  Which ones matter? If tortuosity alone drives the gain, or if angle encoding is responsible, that tells the reader something specific about crack physics. Without this, the edge
  feature contribution is a black box.

  Fix: Run GINE with edge features dropped one at a time (or in groups — geometry vs thickness vs angle). Even a 3-way split (no edge features, geometry only, full features) would
  satisfy this.
